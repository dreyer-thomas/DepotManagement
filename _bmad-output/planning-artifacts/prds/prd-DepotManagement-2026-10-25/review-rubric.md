# PRD Quality Review — DepotManagement

## Overall verdict
This PRD is short and focused, and it is honest about scope. Its thesis is clear: reach parity with what Tommy actually uses in Parqet, with correctness valued over features. The scope and success metrics follow from that thesis, and the cuts (no broker sync, no tax view, finanzen.net zero stays manual) come with stated reasons. It is safe to hand to architecture now. Before epic/story breakdown, three calculation rules need a line or two each, because the "Correctness over features" principle (§5) depends on them: the cost-basis method for realized Gain/Loss, the matching rule for duplicate detection, and how values are computed when a Security has gaps in its Price history.

## Decision-readiness — strong
Decisions are stated as decisions, and each comes with its reason. The PRD rejects automated sync because "the one sync Tommy uses today regularly produces errors that need manual cleanup" (§1). It keeps finanzen.net zero manual in v1 and names document import for it as a later candidate (FR-4). It accepts manual Prices as the fallback for certificates and crypto "given their small share" (FR-9 Notes). It shows TTWROR and XIRR side by side and explains why neither replaces the other (FR-12 Notes). The time-critical step is called out in bold: "The export must be taken before the Parqet subscription is cancelled" (FR-15 Notes).

The two Open Questions (§9) are real questions, and they are correctly handed to architecture. Both bear on feasibility, though: the Price Source question is also the §5 `[ASSUMPTION]` that the zero-cost goal rests on. The heading says they "must be resolved before implementation", which is the right gate. It would help to say what happens if the answer is "no adequate free source".

### Findings
- **medium** No fallback position if Open Question 1 comes back negative (§9, §5 Cost) — The "No paid services required" constraint and FR-7/FR-8 both assume a free Price Source covers German-listed ETFs. The PRD does not say whether Tommy would accept a small paid tier or a lower Price frequency instead. *Fix:* Add one sentence of fallback, e.g. "If no free source exists, a paid source up to €X/month is acceptable" or "daily end-of-day Prices from scraping/an exchange feed are sufficient".

## Substance over theater — strong
There are no personas, no claims of differentiation, and no vision boilerplate. The Vision (§1) is specific to this product: it names the Parqet features in use, the sync pain, and the subscription cost. The NFRs are mostly specific to the product: EUR only, the Nginx/TLS target, no personal data in the public repo, and "a wrong value is worse than a missing feature". The JTBD list (§2.1) maps cleanly onto the feature groups. The research and the analyzed real export (addendum §A1) show that Discovery happened.

The one weak spot is "Responsiveness: Usable in a desktop browser and on a phone browser" (§5). It is an adjective without a bound, but for a solo tool it is acceptable.

## Strategic coherence — strong
The thesis is explicit: parity with Tommy's actual Parqet usage, then owning the roadmap (§1). Feature selection follows it. Each feature group corresponds to a Parqet capability in use, and things never used are cut with reasons (§6.2, e.g. the tax view "never used in Parqet"). The success metrics validate the thesis rather than activity. SM-1 (cancel Parqet and use only DepotManagement for 3 months) tests parity. SM-2 (values reconcile with broker statements) tests correctness. SM-3 (under 10 minutes of monthly import) tests "little effort". A counter-metric is present (SM-C1). The MVP scope is clearly problem-solving / replacement parity.

### Findings
- **low** SM-C1 is a rule, not a metric (§8) — "Do not add Brokers or features beyond Tommy's own use" cannot be measured. As a scope guard it is fine. *Fix:* Either rename it to a scope principle or make it observable, e.g. "no feature merged without a named personal use".

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Where FRs have consequences, they are good and testable. Examples: "Importing the same document twice books nothing new" (FR-6), "Positions and invested capital per Depot match Parqet as of the export date" (FR-15), and the 8-decimal quantities (FR-2). However, FR-5, FR-8, FR-9, FR-10, FR-11, FR-13, FR-14 and FR-16 have no consequences. Most are self-evident enough for a solo build. The gaps below are the ones where an engineer would have to invent a financial rule, and a wrong guess silently breaks SM-2.

### Findings
- **high** Cost-basis method for realized Gain/Loss is undefined (FR-12, Glossary "Gain/Loss", FR-3) — FR-12 requires realized vs unrealized Gain/Loss, but nothing says whether sells consume lots FIFO or at average cost. The two methods give different realized Gain/Loss and different remaining cost basis after partial sells. The addendum notes that Parqet's `realizedgains` is FIFO and proposes it as a reconciliation check, so FIFO is implied but never decided. The Glossary also leaves open whether taxes count against Gain/Loss ("minus costs" — are fees and taxes both costs?). *Fix:* Add to the Glossary or FR-12: "Cost basis per Position uses FIFO (matching Parqet and German tax practice); fees are included in cost basis; taxes are [included/excluded] in Gain/Loss."
- **high** Duplicate-detection matching rule is undefined (FR-6, FR-15) — "detects Transactions that were already booked (from earlier imports or manual entry)" does not say what counts as the same Transaction. Two cases make this hard. First, robo-advisor savings plans produce repeated, near-identical buys (§4.2 Notes), which risks false positives. Second, migrated Parqet rows have rounding mismatches against the broker PDFs (addendum: `123,456` vs `123,46`), which risks false negatives. A wrong rule either double-books or silently drops real Transactions, which breaks SM-2 and the "trust the numbers" JTBD. *Fix:* Specify the match key and tolerance, e.g. "same Depot, Security, type, trade date, quantity; amount within €0.01". Also say whether a suspected duplicate is auto-skipped or shown in review (FR-5) for Tommy to decide.
- **high** Valuation with missing or partial Price history is undefined (FR-8, FR-9, FR-11) — FR-8 requires daily history "back to its first Transaction". FR-9 allows manual Prices when no source covers a Security, and FR-9 Notes expect this for certificates/crypto. Hand-entering daily history is not realistic, so the chart and TTWROR for those Positions need a rule. Options include carrying forward the last known Price, using transaction prices as anchor points, or interpolating. *Fix:* Add a consequence: "Where no Price exists for a date, the last known Price (manual, transaction, or sourced) is carried forward; such Positions are marked as estimated in charts."
- **medium** "Stale" Price threshold not defined (FR-7) — "flags stale Prices" is not testable. *Fix:* e.g. "A Price is stale if older than 1 trading day (stocks/ETFs) or 24h (crypto)."
- **medium** Corporate action detail is thin (FR-3) — ISIN changes are described as "one Security replaced by another, cost basis carried over", with no mention of a quantity ratio. ISIN changes often coincide with a reverse split or exchange ratio. Splits need a ratio and an effective date. *Fix:* Add "ISIN change records old/new quantity (ratio may be ≠ 1); split records ratio and effective date; history before the date is shown split-adjusted."
- **medium** Export/restore semantics unspecified (FR-16) — "an open file format and restore from it" does not say whether restore replaces all data or merges, or whether manual Prices and archived Depots are included. Without that, round-trip fidelity is untestable. *Fix:* Add the consequence "Export then restore into an empty instance reproduces identical Depots, Transactions, manual Prices, and evaluations."
- **low** News freshness and quality are adjectives (FR-14) — "recent" and "Quality should be comparable to Parqet's" are not testable. This is acceptable for a solo build, since the source is still open. *Fix:* Bound "recent" (e.g. last 14 days, max N per Security).

## Scope honesty — strong
Omissions are explicit. §6.2 lists the out-of-scope items with reasons, §7 Non-Goals does real work (not trading, not tax reporting, not SaaS), §5 states EUR only, FR-13 marks ETF look-through out of scope, and FR-4 explicitly leaves finanzen.net zero import for later. There are 3 `[ASSUMPTION]` tags and 2 Open Questions, which is light for a hobby PRD. There are no `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts, which is acceptable at this size.

### Findings
- **medium** Security transfers between Depots are not addressed (FR-15, addendum §A1) — Migration treats Security `TransferOut`/`TransferIn` pairs as ISIN changes. Real Depot-to-Depot transfers, or free deliveries in or out (Depotübertrag), also appear as TransferIn/TransferOut, and the Transaction type list (Glossary) has no transfer type. It is unclear whether this is out of scope or simply not considered. *Fix:* State whether Depot transfers are v1 (add a transfer Transaction type that keeps cost basis) or a `[NON-GOAL for MVP]`. Also say how migration distinguishes an unpaired Security TransferIn from an ISIN change.
- **low** Migration evidence covers only one of three Depots (FR-15, addendum §A1) — Only the finanzen.net zero export was analyzed. The comdirect/cominvest (robo-advisor) and ROBIN exports may contain other types (e.g. splits, savings-plan rows) that have not been seen. *Fix:* Add an `[ASSUMPTION]` that the other exports share the observed format, or analyze them before the subscription ends.

## Downstream usability — adequate
The Glossary is present and mostly used consistently: Depot, Position, Cash Account, Price Source and Period are capitalized and used in their defined senses. FR IDs 1–18 are contiguous and unique. UJs have a protagonist (Tommy) and each is referenced from feature descriptions. Cross-references resolve (FR-6 ↔ FR-15, FR-17 ↔ FR-15, OQ 2 ↔ FR-14). The calculation-rule gaps listed under Done-ness are the main things architecture would otherwise have to guess.

### Findings
- **low** FR ordering vs numbering (§4.6, §4.7) — Cash Account (FR-17/18) comes after Data Ownership (FR-15/16) but is referenced earlier by FR-15. This is harmless, but an epic split by section may want Cash Account sequenced before migration. *Fix:* None needed. Optionally note the dependency (FR-15 depends on FR-17, FR-3) for the epic breakdown.

## Shape fit — strong
The PRD fits a hobby/solo, chain-top capability spec: three short UJs that actually drive features (UJ-2's failed-layout edge case drives FR-4's per-file status), no personas, operational success metrics, and implementation detail correctly pushed to the addendum. The length is close to the two-page target. It is neither over- nor under-formalized.
